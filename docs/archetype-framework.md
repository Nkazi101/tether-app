## Goal

Tether should talk about history and current events in a way that feels smart without sounding like a college seminar.

The model below keeps the deep structure we need for matching and analysis, but uses language regular people can understand right away.

## Core Idea

Tether should use a hybrid model with two layers:

1. Event archetypes explain the kind of situation we are looking at.
2. Persona archetypes explain the kind of people inside that situation and how they usually act under pressure.

This means:

- events answer: "What kind of pattern is this?"
- personas answer: "What kind of player is this?"
- structural roles answer: "Where are they standing in the system?"
- play-out answer: "How does this usually unfold?"

## Product Rule

Use plain-English labels in the UI.

Keep the deeper language, legacy labels, and biblical inspiration under the hood when useful for editorial, ranking, or analysis.

If a label sounds too academic, too churchy, or too vague, it should not be the main thing users see.

## Layer 1: Event Archetypes

These are the system-level patterns.

| Internal Slug | Public Label | What It Means In Plain English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `strategic-competition` | Big Power Showdown | Two strong players see each other as a real threat, and the fight keeps growing. |
| `regulatory-crackdown` | The Giant Gets Checked | A company, leader, or system gets so powerful that someone steps in to slow it down. |
| `labor-dispute` | Workers Push Back | People doing the work decide the deal is no longer fair. |
| `platform-transition` | The Ground Shifts | A new technology or platform changes the rules faster than the old system can adapt. |
| `leadership-change` | New Boss, New Rules | A new leader changes expectations, incentives, and the tone of the whole system. |
| `dynastic-rise-or-decline` | Rise or Fall of a Dynasty | A long-dominant force keeps winning, starts slipping, or gets replaced. |
| `distribution-shift` | Control of the Audience Changes Hands | The path to customers, fans, or voters moves to a new gatekeeper. |
| `scandal-cycle` | Scandal and Blowback | A public mistake or shame event turns into a larger wave of punishment and overreaction. |
| `cultural-revival` | The Old Thing Comes Back | A style, symbol, or idea returns because it now means something new. |
| `consumer-trend-swing` | Everyone Wants It, Then They Don't | Tastes change fast and the market follows the mood. |
| `resource-shock` | The Supply Squeeze | A key input becomes scarce, expensive, or politically dangerous. |
| `legitimacy-crisis` | People Stop Believing the System | The public stops trusting the people in charge, and the old story stops working. |

## Layer 2: Persona Archetypes

These are the people-level patterns.

They are biblical-inspired, but written in everyday language so they feel open to everybody.

### `threatened-king`

- Public label: The Leader Who Feels the Ground Moving
- Biblical inspiration: Saul
- Core drive: stay in control
- Under pressure: tightens control, trusts fewer people, punishes disloyalty
- Common tactic: uses the system to protect the system
- Typical downfall: speeds up the very loss of trust they fear

### `anointed-outsider`

- Public label: The Underdog on the Rise
- Biblical inspiration: David
- Core drive: prove they belong at the top
- Under pressure: gets bolder, more symbolic, and more direct
- Common tactic: turns contrast into momentum
- Typical downfall: wins power, then starts acting like the old guard

### `exile-strategist`

- Public label: The Outsider Who Knows How to Survive
- Biblical inspiration: Joseph
- Core drive: survive, adapt, and quietly gain leverage
- Under pressure: thinks ahead, centralizes decisions, plays the long game
- Common tactic: solves the crisis better than the insiders
- Typical downfall: saves the system, but makes it more dependent on control from the center

### `reluctant-messenger`

- Public label: The One Who Says What Others Won't
- Biblical inspiration: Moses or Jonah
- Core drive: tell the truth even when it costs them
- Under pressure: hesitates at first, then becomes clearer and firmer
- Common tactic: names the problem nobody wants to say out loud
- Typical downfall: gets isolated, ignored, or used as a symbol more than a leader

### `court-insider`

- Public label: The Person Inside the Room
- Biblical inspiration: Esther or Daniel
- Core drive: shape outcomes from the inside
- Under pressure: stays careful, patient, and strategic
- Common tactic: timing, access, quiet persuasion
- Typical downfall: gets trapped by loyalty, silence, or proximity to power

### `wise-builder`

- Public label: The Builder of the New Order
- Biblical inspiration: Solomon
- Core drive: build something lasting, orderly, and impressive
- Under pressure: over-designs, over-centralizes, and trusts their own brilliance too much
- Common tactic: builds systems that make everyone else play by new rules
- Typical downfall: creates something strong that later turns brittle

### `betrayer-within`

- Public label: The Insider Who Breaks Ranks
- Biblical inspiration: Judas
- Core drive: act on private grievance, disillusionment, or self-interest
- Under pressure: defects at the worst possible moment for the group
- Common tactic: uses trust and access as leverage
- Typical downfall: becomes the face of a fracture that was already there

### `restorer`

- Public label: The One Who Puts Things Back Together
- Biblical inspiration: Nehemiah and post-exile rebuilders
- Core drive: rebuild after failure or drift
- Under pressure: gets disciplined, practical, and unsentimental
- Common tactic: focuses on repair, boundaries, and durable order
- Typical downfall: rebuilds stability but leaves little room for experimentation or mercy

## Structural Roles

These explain where the actor sits in the system.

| Role | Plain Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `incumbent` | The person or group already in charge |
| `challenger` | The person or group trying to take that place |
| `broker` | The deal-maker between sides |
| `insider` | Someone inside the institution but not at the top |
| `outsider` | Someone outside the core system looking in or pushing in |
| `witness` | Someone whose voice gives moral clarity, public attention, or social permission |

## How The Hybrid Works

Every major actor in a story gets:

- an event archetype for the situation
- a persona archetype for the type of person they are in that episode
- a structural role for their place in the system
- a play-out for how they are likely to act as pressure rises

## Common Pairings

These are the most natural combinations for the current Tether model.

### Big Power Showdown

- Most common personas: The Leader Who Feels the Ground Moving, The Underdog on the Rise, The Person Inside the Room, The Outsider Who Knows How to Survive
- Typical play-out: each side reads the other as a threat, insiders harden, and compromise gets framed as weakness

### The Giant Gets Checked

- Most common personas: The Builder of the New Order, The Leader Who Feels the Ground Moving, The One Who Puts Things Back Together, The Person Inside the Room
- Typical play-out: a dominant player grows too powerful, then the system decides correction is safer than letting them keep expanding

### Workers Push Back

- Most common personas: The Underdog on the Rise, The Leader Who Feels the Ground Moving, The One Who Says What Others Won't, The Insider Who Breaks Ranks
- Typical play-out: ordinary grievances become organized pressure, then leadership must either bargain, delay, or crack down

### The Ground Shifts

- Most common personas: The Builder of the New Order, The Outsider Who Knows How to Survive, The Leader Who Feels the Ground Moving, The One Who Puts Things Back Together
- Typical play-out: innovators move fast, incumbents deny the depth of change, and repair comes only after disruption is already underway

### New Boss, New Rules

- Most common personas: The Underdog on the Rise, The Person Inside the Room, The One Who Puts Things Back Together, The Leader Who Feels the Ground Moving
- Typical play-out: a new leader promises renewal, then discovers the old system is harder to move than the campaign story suggested

### Rise or Fall of a Dynasty

- Most common personas: The Leader Who Feels the Ground Moving, The Underdog on the Rise, The Builder of the New Order
- Typical play-out: long success breeds habit, habit breeds rigidity, and the next contender wins by seeing the world more clearly

### Control of the Audience Changes Hands

- Most common personas: The Builder of the New Order, The Outsider Who Knows How to Survive, The Leader Who Feels the Ground Moving, The Insider Who Breaks Ranks
- Typical play-out: the path to attention or customers moves elsewhere, and old gatekeepers learn too late that owning content is not the same as owning distribution

### Scandal and Blowback

- Most common personas: The Insider Who Breaks Ranks, The Person Inside the Room, The One Who Says What Others Won't, The Leader Who Feels the Ground Moving
- Typical play-out: one event becomes a test of values, institutions overcorrect, and the response grows bigger than the original offense

### The Old Thing Comes Back

- Most common personas: The Builder of the New Order, The One Who Says What Others Won't, The Outsider Who Knows How to Survive
- Typical play-out: the past returns because people are searching for stability, meaning, or a fresh way to signal identity

### Everyone Wants It, Then They Don't

- Most common personas: The Builder of the New Order, The Underdog on the Rise, The Leader Who Feels the Ground Moving
- Typical play-out: hype moves faster than loyalty, and yesterday's status symbol becomes tomorrow's punchline

### The Supply Squeeze

- Most common personas: The Outsider Who Knows How to Survive, The One Who Puts Things Back Together, The Leader Who Feels the Ground Moving
- Typical play-out: scarcity exposes what the system was taking for granted, then power moves toward whoever can still keep things moving

### People Stop Believing the System

- Most common personas: The Leader Who Feels the Ground Moving, The Underdog on the Rise, The One Who Says What Others Won't, The Insider Who Breaks Ranks, The One Who Puts Things Back Together
- Typical play-out: trust fades before authority does, then everything changes very quickly once enough people stop pretending the old order still works

## Writing Rules For The Product

- Lead with the public label, not the internal slug.
- Prefer words that people can feel on first read.
- Avoid terms that need a history degree to decode.
- Avoid turning persona labels into permanent identity claims.
- Describe who a person is in this episode, not who they are forever.
- Do not moralize too early. Show the pattern before judging the person.

## Suggested Data Shape

```ts
type EventTypeSlug =
  | "strategic-competition"
  | "regulatory-crackdown"
  | "labor-dispute"
  | "platform-transition"
  | "leadership-change"
  | "dynastic-rise-or-decline"
  | "distribution-shift"
  | "scandal-cycle"
  | "cultural-revival"
  | "consumer-trend-swing"
  | "resource-shock"
  | "legitimacy-crisis";

type PersonaArchetypeSlug =
  | "threatened-king"
  | "anointed-outsider"
  | "exile-strategist"
  | "reluctant-messenger"
  | "court-insider"
  | "wise-builder"
  | "betrayer-within"
  | "restorer";

type StructuralRole =
  | "incumbent"
  | "challenger"
  | "broker"
  | "insider"
  | "outsider"
  | "witness";

type ActorArchetype = {
  name: string;
  structuralRole: StructuralRole;
  personaArchetype: PersonaArchetypeSlug;
  publicPersonaLabel: string;
  coreDrive: string;
  stressBehavior: string;
  commonTactic: string;
  likelyDownfall: string;
  playOut: string;
};

type EventArchetypeRecord = {
  eventTypeSlug: EventTypeSlug;
  publicEventLabel: string;
  plainEnglishMeaning: string;
  likelyPersonas: PersonaArchetypeSlug[];
};
```

## Final Recommendation

Use the current event model as the backbone.

Add persona archetypes as a second layer for people.

Keep the biblical inspiration in the bones of the system, but keep the language everyday, visual, and human in the product itself.
